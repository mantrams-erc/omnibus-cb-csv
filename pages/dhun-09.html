9. Dhun: Repetition and Afterlives

Dhun is repetition, vibration, persistence.

Not only the repetition of words, but of gestures, movements, sounds, surfaces, routes, images, and returns. Across Vārāṇasī and beyond, the name of Rām moves continuously through bodies, media, objects, shrines, songs, fires, and everyday acts of circulation. Sometimes loudly amplified. Sometimes barely audible. Sometimes intensely embodied. Sometimes dispersed almost to abstraction.

What remains after repetition is not always a stable meaning.

Names fade from walls and are written again. Posters tear and are replaced. Songs are forgotten, improvised, recorded, and recirculated. Digital images disappear into endless feeds while older devotional forms continue to persist through touch, memory, inscription, and sound. The name survives not through permanence, but through continuous acts of renewal and return.

This exhibition and the accompanying essays have approached mantra not as a singular object or isolated utterance, but as something unfolding across sensory, material, social, political, and devotional worlds. What emerges is necessarily partial: a series of rendezvous shaped by movement through field sites, archives, performances, recordings, shrines, conversations, and acts of repetition that continue beyond the frame of the exhibition itself.

Dhun lingers after the sound fades.
